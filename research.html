Human-AI interaction: resource constraints, trust and judgment

AI Usage Limits and Token Anxiety: The Effects of Resource Visibility on Model Selection, Prompting, and Trust in AI Recommendations Preprint Tan, F., Avula, S., & Nov, O. AI providers increasingly impose token budgets and usage limits, shifting resource management onto users. Making those limits visible raised token anxiety and model-switching without improving resource utilization or prompting strategy.
LLMsModel selectionTrust
The Evaluation–Contestation Gap: Users Rarely Contest Misfitting Conversational AI Advice and Adapt It Themselves Preprint Tan, F., Noh, H., Messerschmidt, M. A., Das Swain, V., & Nov, O. Conversational AI interfaces afford users the means to contest advice in-session, yet users rarely contest misfitting advice, leaving signals of mismatch unrecorded in conversation logs. Afforded contestability does not ensure realized contestability.
LLMsContestabilityReliance
The Impact of Response Latency and Task Type on Human-LLM Interaction and Perception ACM CHI 2026 Tan, F., Messerschmidt, M. A., Yin, W., & Nov, O. LLM products assume faster responses are better. This work asks how time-to-first-token (TTFT) latency shapes the way users interact with an LLM and judge its output.
LLMsLatency
Counting the Wait: Effects of Temporal Feedback on Downstream Task Performance and Perceived Wait-Time Experience during System-Imposed Delays ACM CHI 2026 Tan, F., & Nov, O. Prior work examines how temporal feedback shapes perceived waiting, but few studies ask how it affects downstream task performance and affective experience. Remaining-Time feedback increased frustration relative to Elapsed-Time, with no differences in post-wait performance.

LLM-assisted writing often fails to capture the subtle tones essential to interpersonal communication. PersonaMail combines structured communication factor exploration, granular editing controls, and adaptive reuse of successful strategies.

Signals of Aggression: Modelling Multimodal Cues and Perceptual Effects in Virtual Agents ACM CHI 2026 Ong, S., Koh, A., Cai, S., Tan, F., Chia, P. & Khoo, E. T. Intelligent virtual agents (IVAs) must convincingly convey aggression in applications such as customer service and conflict training, yet prior work has focused on basic emotions and unimodal cues. Towards Adversarial Collaboration in HCI Research ACM CHI 2026 Extended Abstracts Nov, O., Knijnenburg, B. P., & Tan, F. HCI research holds conflicting accounts of how and why people interact with technologies, such as algorithm aversion versus appreciation. Adversarial collaboration has rival researchers jointly design and run experiments under mutually pre-agreed protocols to adjudicate such disagreements.

Attention, curiosity and learning

Curious Shorts: Curiosity-Driven Exploration and Learning on Short-Form Video Platforms ACM CHI 2025 Tan, F., Ram, A., Messerschmidt, M. A., Dissanayake, H. A., & Nanayakkara, S. Short-form video platforms captivate users, but their potential for incidental learning remains underexplored. Curiosity nudges in the form of question-driven prompts boosted curiosity and intentional viewing, though learning benefits diminished amid entertainment videos.
