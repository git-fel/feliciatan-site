I am a PhD candidate at the NYU Tandon School of Engineering, conducting research in human-computer interaction (HCI). My path to doctoral research was shaped by formative years at Yale-NUS College in Singapore, followed by work in venture capital and early-stage startups. Within an industry defined by the rapid turnover of ideas and continual chase for the next big thing, I found myself most energized by deeper questions underlying how technologies shape human thinking and experience.

HCI offered a natural home for this curiosity, for it is a field that brings together the science (empirical understanding) and invention (engineering/design) of technology.

My research today focuses on human-AI interaction, specifically, how resource constraints in AI tools (e.g., token limits, latency) shape model selection, workflows and human judgment. I try to surface these tradeoffs and design systems around them.

Outside my core research, I care about building AI literacy among older adults, and protecting vulnerable communities from AI-enabled scams. If you have ideas or are building in this space, I'd love to connect.

Recent research

Preprint AI Usage Limits and Token Anxiety AI providers increasingly impose token budgets and usage limits, shifting resource management onto users. Making those limits visible raised token anxiety and model-switching without improving resource utilization or prompting strategy.
CHI '26 Response Latency and Task Type in Human-LLM Interaction LLM products assume faster responses are better. This work asks how time-to-first-token (TTFT) latency shapes the way users interact with an LLM and judge its output.
IUI '26 PersonaMail LLM-assisted writing often fails to capture the subtle tones essential to interpersonal communication. PersonaMail combines structured communication factor exploration, granular editing controls, and adaptive reuse of successful strategies.
CHI '26 Counting the Wait Prior work examines how temporal feedback shapes perceived waiting, but few studies ask how it affects downstream task performance and affective experience. Remaining-Time feedback increased frustration relative to Elapsed-Time, with no differences in post-wait performance.
