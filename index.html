I am a PhD candidate at the NYU Tandon School of Engineering, conducting research in human-computer interaction (HCI). My path to doctoral research was shaped by formative years at Yale-NUS College in Singapore, followed by work in venture capital and early-stage startups. Within an industry defined by the rapid turnover of ideas and continual chase for the next big thing, I found myself most energized by deeper questions underlying how technologies shape human thinking and experience.

HCI offered a natural home for this curiosity, for it is a field that brings together the science (empirical understanding) and invention (engineering/design) of technology.

My research today focuses on human-AI interaction, specifically, how resource constraints in AI tools (e.g., token limits, latency) shape model selection, workflows and human judgment. I try to surface these tradeoffs and design systems around them.

Outside my core research, I care about building AI literacy among older adults, and protecting vulnerable communities from AI-enabled scams. If you have ideas or are building in this space, I'd love to connect.

Recent research

Preprint AI Usage Limits and Token Anxiety A survey of frequent AI users (N = 953) and an experimental evaluation (N = 135) found that resource visibility significantly increased token anxiety, model-switching, and exploratory behavior without improving resource utilization or prompting strategy. Resource visibility does not directly translate to resource manageability.
CHI '26 Response Latency and Task Type in Human-LLM Interaction A controlled experiment varying time-to-first-token latency (2, 9, 20 seconds) across Creation and Advice tasks. User interaction behaviors were robust to latency, yet participants who experienced 2-second latencies rated the LLM’s outputs less thoughtful and useful than those who experienced 9- or 20-second latencies.
IUI '26 PersonaMail An email-writing system with structured communication factor exploration, granular editing controls, and adaptive reuse of successful strategies. Compared against standard LLM interfaces in a 16-participant evaluation, it showed improved efficiency in both immediate and repeated use, alongside higher user satisfaction.
CHI '26 Counting the Wait An online experiment (n = 425) on temporal feedback during system-imposed waits. Remaining-Time feedback increased frustration relative to Elapsed-Time, and No Time Display made waits feel longer, without differences in post-wait task performance.
